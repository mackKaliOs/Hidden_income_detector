The problem

In many support, asset, and financial-disclosure cases, reported income tells only part of the story. Hidden compensation, business control, operational authority, and benefit access often appear long before bank records do.

Self-reported income is incomplete

Traditional case strategy often starts too late—after expensive discovery, subpoenas, and delay.

Contradictions appear first

Role, control, time allocation, insurance activity, business involvement, and benefit access can reveal capacity before money trails are produced.

Courts need structure, not speculation

Judges respond to consistency, chronology, and explainable reasoning—not suspicion alone.

The solution

Hidden Income Detector is a contradiction-based inference system that converts behavioral and public-record signals into structured evidence outputs suitable for litigation support.

Four-variable model

P = (w₁Id + w₂Lg + w₃C) / (1 + w₄Ex)

  • Identity: claimed role vs actual role
  • Legitimacy: consistency of statements across contexts
  • Control: operational authority and dependency patterns
  • Extraction: benefit or access exceeding declared capacity

Litigation-ready outputs

  • Scoring matrices
  • Chronological contradiction timelines
  • Exhibit packet PDFs
  • Reusable, reproducible analysis workflow

This is not a bank-data scraping tool. It is a structured contradiction analysis engine aligned with how courts evaluate credibility and earning capacity.

How it works

The framework starts with claimed status, then measures whether the claimed reality can logically coexist with observed authority, behavior, and benefit.

1

Collect non-financial inputs

Public records, metadata, behavioral signals, role descriptions, insurance activity, and timeline events.

2

Score contradiction vectors

Identity mismatch, statement inconsistency, control differential, and extraction/benefit gap are weighted and scored.

3

Generate evidentiary structure

Outputs become readable, repeatable, and court-usable—flowchart, scoring matrix, and chronological evidence table.

4

Support litigation strategy

Use the packet to support disclosure requests, imputation arguments, credibility challenges, and targeted discovery.
